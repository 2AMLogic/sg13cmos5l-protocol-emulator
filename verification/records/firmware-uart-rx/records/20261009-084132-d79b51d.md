- **Record ID**: 20261009-084132-d79b51d
- **Claim**: Four **committed** firmware programs, loaded over the real serial
  load-phase pins onto `tt_um_2amlogic_protocol_emulator` and executed by the ISA
  core (`rtl/protocol_core.v`), close the two UART gaps `uart_tx`'s record
  (`verification/records/firmware-uart/`) left open, under Icarus and with no
  UART hardware: (1) the **receive direction** -- `firmware/asm/uart_rx.asm`
  (50 cycles/bit), `uart_rx_115200.asm` (434) and `uart_rx_9600.asm` (5,208) --
  receive every byte the **independent** model (`verification/reference_models/uart.py`)
  sends, with bounded edge jitter and **+-2 % per-bit rate error**, with sample
  instants measured **at the pins** to be exactly one bit period apart; and (2) the
  **low-baud end** of target-spec row 1/10's 9,600-1,000,000 baud range -- the
  nested-`WAIT` outer-loop case a single 8-bit `WAIT` cannot reach -- for both
  directions (`uart_rx_9600`, and `uart_tx_9600.asm`, whose frames
  `UartDecoder` recovers and `check_frame` grades at 0.000 % drift). Scope
  boundary: UART only, 8N1, Icarus RTL simulation only. It claims **no clock
  frequency or baud rate** (see below), no SPI/I2C, no gate-level or
  post-layout result, no area, no silicon, and **does not close row 10**:
  the receiver's tolerance is shown at +-2 % per-bit, which is *not* the same
  quantity as row 10's cumulative-frame-drift bound on a transmitter (see
  "Two bounds").

- **Result**: **PASS -- 9/9 cases** (`artifacts/20261009-084132-d79b51d/results_icarus.xml`,
  console `test-icarus-console.txt`, klt JSON `klt-functional-verification.json`).
  - **Flow named: all numbers here come from the klt/Icarus RTL cocotb flow
    only.** No LibreLane/Tiny Tapeout-flow number is reported or implied.
  - **Receive, bytes**: every frame received correctly at 50, 434 and 5,208
    cycles/bit, across nominal, +-10 % edge jitter, **+-2 %** per-bit rate
    error, +-2 % with jitter, +-0.2 %, and idle gaps of 0, 0.5, 1 and 2.5 bit
    periods (13 / 13 / 6 frames per program). The stimulus is the model's
    `encode_frame` / `encode_slow_frame`; the received byte is `uo_out`.
  - **Receive, cycle-exact timing, measured at the pins**: the firmware raises
    `uio_out[0]` for a few cycles after each `IN` of the line. Across all
    frames the 9 marks of a frame (8 data + stop) are **exactly P cycles apart
    (P = 50, 434, 5,208), zero violations**; the first data sample lands
    74-76 cycles after the start edge first became visible at 50 cycles/bit
    (static window [74, 76]: 3-cycle start-detect poll granularity), 650-652
    at 434 (window [650, 652]) and 7,812-7,813 at 5,208 (window
    [7,812, 7,814]). Sample position inside the sender's bit (0.5 = centre):
    nominal 0.466-0.518 at 50 cycles/bit and 0.498-0.502 at 434 / 0.500 at
    5,208; with +2 % error the later samples walk to 0.276-0.472, with -2 %
    to 0.512-0.695 (all inside the bit, as the bench asserts).
  - **Tolerance sweep (informational, 4 probe bytes per rate, 50 cycles/bit,
    12-bit idle between frames)**: rate x1.02: 4/4, x0.98: 4/4, x1.04: 4/4,
    x0.96: 4/4, x1.06: 3/4, x0.94: 2/4, x1.08: 1/4, x0.92: 0/4, x1.10: 1/4,
    x0.90: 0/4. So the receiver tolerates roughly +-4 % per-bit error on
    every probe and degrades past that; the asserted bounds are +-2 % pass and
    +-10 % not-all-pass. (x1.10 = 1/4 is a chance coincidence of the probe
    bytes, which is why the control asserts "not all correct".)
  - **Low-baud transmit**: `uart_tx_9600` frames `0x5a`, `0xa5` decoded by
    `UartDecoder` at 5,208 cycles/bit; `check_frame` True, drift 0.000 % on
    both; every intra-frame TX edge interval a whole multiple of 5,208 cycles
    (`[10416, 5208, 5208, 10416, 5208, 5208, 5208]` and
    `[5208, 5208, 5208, 5208, 10416, 5208, 5208]`).
  - **Two bounds, not one.** Row 10's adopted bound is cumulative per-frame
    drift <= ~2 % of a bit period, measured by the model on a transmitter's last
    in-frame edge. Over a 10-bit frame that is roughly **0.2 % per-bit** rate
    error. The issue asks the *receiver* to tolerate **+-2 % per-bit**, ten times
    that, which is outside what `check_frame` accepts of a transmitter: the
    log shows `check_frame(stimulus)=False` for every +-2 % and jittered frame
    and `True` for nominal and +-0.2 % frames. The bench asserts the receiver at
    +-2 % and the model's verdict at nominal/+-0.2 %, and reports both. This is
    a **receiver-tolerance** result; it is *not* a claim that a +-2 %-error
    transmitter meets row 10.
  - **Negative controls -- all proven to fail**: (1) rate error beyond tolerance
    (+-10 %) returns wrong bytes (control asserts not-all-correct); (2) the
    model's 1.025x transmitter decodes to the right byte but fails
    `check_frame` at 22.500 % (bound 2.0 %); (3) the DUT's own captured
    `uart_tx_9600` waveform re-timed 1.025x fails `check_frame` at 22.500 % and
    20.000 %; (4) the cycle-exactness checker rejects a real capture with one
    sample mark displaced by one cycle (`[(3, 51), (4, 49)]`); (5) a stop bit
    held low sets the framing-error flag (`uio_out[1]`) and the following good
    frame is still received and clears it; (6) a **mutated-firmware probe**,
    archived as `artifacts/20261009-084132-d79b51d/negative-control-mistimed-firmware.json` and
    `-console.txt`: the data-to-stop `WAIT 35` in `uart_rx.asm` was changed to
    `WAIT 36` (one extra cycle between samples 7 and 8), the program
    re-assembled and the bench re-run -- it **fails 3 of 9** cases
    (`test_uart_rx_1mbaud_class`, `test_framing_error_flag_and_recovery`,
    `test_negative_control_spacing_checker_can_fail`) on
    `sample spacing != 50: [(7, 51)]`. The probe was then reverted
    (`git checkout firmware/`; `asm.py --check` confirms the committed artifacts
    are byte-identical to the ones hashed above). Those two files are the only
    ones produced from a deliberately mutated source and are labelled as such.
  - **ISA finding -- the outer-loop shape.** A `WAIT imm8` is 1-256 cycles, so
    bit periods above 256 use `LDI n; WAIT 255; SUB; BNZ`: **258 cycles per
    pass** (256 + `SUB` + `BNZ`), not the 259 a first derivation assumed. The first
    bench run caught exactly that (sample spacing 5,188 vs 5,208, TX drift
    3.456 %) before anything was committed; the generator's constant was fixed
    and everything above is from the corrected programs. The nested loop needs
    the fourth register, so the 8 bit blocks are **unrolled**: 132-177 words per
    program, well inside the 256-word store but leaving no room for two
    protocols in one image at 5,208 cycles/bit -- the sketch's "all three
    protocols coexist" narrative is again stretched, not broken. No ISA defect
    and no spec change was needed.
  - **Receiver re-arm limit (a finding, not a failure).** After the stop-bit
    sample the receiver re-arms start detect 7 cycles later (0.14 bit at 50
    cycles/bit). A back-to-back frame (zero idle) from a sender running >2 % fast
    could begin before re-arm; the bench keeps zero idle only on nominal frames
    and uses >= 0.5-bit idle for rate-error frames. Late detection only shifts
    the sample points; a missed start edge is not exercised.
  - **No baud rate is measured, and no microsecond figure is a measurement.**
    Row 4's clock is unconfirmed on this flow (STA against this core is blocked,
    `verification/records/sta-corner-sweep/`; it closes only on the LibreLane leg,
    `verification/records/librelane-corner-timing/`). 5,208 cycles x 20 ns = ~9,600
    baud and 434 cycles x 20 ns = ~115,207 baud are arithmetic at that
    unconfirmed clock. The claim is the cycle count.
  - **Start-edge handshakes are data-dependent by nature.** Each receiver draws
    exactly three assembler lint warnings (the `sync` and `poll` loops and the
    single post-stop re-arm branch), asserted by the bench; none lies between
    samples, and the transmitter draws none.
  - **Calibration**: `test_pin_timing_calibration` measures, with a
    3-instruction program, that an `IN` samples the line value present just
    before its retiring edge and that an `OUT` is visible at the pin at its own
    retiring edge's end (mark visible 1 edge after the `IN`); the cycle
    arithmetic above is built on that measured fact.
  - **What this record does NOT claim**: no clock frequency or baud rate; not
    row 10 closed (the transmit-side drift bound is measured at 50 and 5,208
    cycles/bit only, 115,200-class transmit is unmeasured); no parity or other
    framing than 8N1; no false-start (glitch) rejection at mid-start-bit (the
    receiver does not re-check the start bit); no SPI/I2C; no gate-level,
    post-layout or silicon result; no area.
