(2) Non-vacuity: all five cover statements reached (`conformant-cover.log`) -- a multi-cycle `WAIT` observed mid-stall and completing its full table latency before advancing, a taken branch to a data-chosen target, a not-taken branch, a branch-to-self holding pc across a genuine 1-cycle retirement, and HALT freezing the core. (4) Negative control: the mutant fixture (one injected data-dependent `WAIT` early-out: a stall cut to 1 cycle when a free data input equals `0xA5`) produces a counterexample in 5 cycles (`mutant-bmc.log`, `mutant-cex.vcd`): `WAIT imm=255` at pc 0 with `mutant_early_data=0xA5` advances after a single cycle while the property's occupancy shadow expects 256 -- precisely DR 0001's "a conditional branch, an early-out ... that terminates on data" defect class. **Two elaboration traps surfaced by this run, both documented in the runner's header and both load-bearing for reproducibility**: (a) bare `opt` after `flatten` on this design replaces the live table-latency cone (`u_property.tbl_lat` and its uses) with `9'x` constants, which `write_smt2` then exposes as free bits -- manifesting as instant bogus counterexamples on the conformant fixture; the explicit opt sub-pass sequence avoids it (verified by RTLIL diff). (b) The frontend's automatic `mem2reg` turns a memory into zero-initialized registers (an all-NOP program that trivially passes and catches nothing); `-nomem2reg` keeps it a `$mem`/free-content model. The runner checks both directions via its positive/negative controls, so a future yosys regression on either trap fails the run loudly instead of silently weakening the proof.
